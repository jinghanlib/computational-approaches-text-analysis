1 · Software QDA

Manual coding with Taguette and QualCoder

What Is Software-Assisted QDA?

Structures what researchers have always done: read closely, annotate, and organize meaning.

Core functions:

  • Code: assign labels to passages
  • Memo: write interpretive notes
  • Query: search across codes and documents
  • Visualize: co-occurrence maps, frequency charts

The software does not find themes. It helps you track and retrieve the themes you identify.

Emphasize that QDA software is a tool for organizing human interpretation, not automating it. The analyst still makes every decision.

AI-Assisted Coding

AI can suggest codes, summarize passages, and group themes.

Best practice:

  • Structure prompts incrementally, one task at a time
  • Review every AI suggestion before accepting it
  • Document your prompts and model used
  • De-identify data before sending to any cloud model
  • Prefer local models for sensitive transcripts

The key tension is between efficiency and interpretive control. AI can speed up initial coding but introduces the risk of accepting suggestions without critical review.

2 · Word Counting

Frequency analysis with Voyant Tools

Pre-Processing Steps

Before counting, text must be cleaned:

  1. Tokenizing: split text into individual words
  2. Normalizing: lowercase, remove punctuation
  3. Filtering stop words: remove “the”, “and”, “is”
  4. Lemmatizing: “promoting”, “promotes”, “promoted” → “promote”

These choices directly shape what the analysis surfaces.

What Frequency Reveals and Conceals

Reveals

  • Recurring concepts
  • Vocabulary differences across speakers
  • Outlier vocabulary

Misses

  • Tone and evaluation
  • Sarcasm, hedging, negation
  • Context and sequence

Frequency is a starting point, not a finding.

3 · Sentiment Analysis

Scoring tone with VADER

What Is Sentiment Analysis?

Assigns a numerical score: positive, negative, or neutral.

VADER works from a lexicon of pre-scored words. Designed for short, informal social media text.

Score Meaning
positive Proportion of tokens with positive valence
negative Proportion of tokens with negative valence
compound −1.0 (most negative) to +1.0 (most positive)

Compound ≥ +0.05 = positive · ≤ −0.05 = negative · between = neutral

The Results

48 of 50 paragraphs score as positive

Compound scores cluster between +0.70 and +0.98

All six speakers have median scores above +0.75

What Does This Mean?

VADER reads honest, trust, values, responsible as positive.

But interview responses use these words to frame complex situations.

The tool cannot distinguish between:

  • “I believe in transparency” (conviction)
  • “I always say I believe in transparency” (performative)
  • “I believe in transparency, but sometimes it’s difficult” (with reservation)

The genre shapes the signal.

This is the key teaching moment for sentiment analysis. The near-uniform positive result is not about the speakers’ sincerity — it reflects the interview genre, where professional self-presentation calls for positive framing.

What the Scores Do Reveal

Even within a uniformly positive distribution:

  • Ben-003 scores negative — he is describing other influencers’ unethical behavior
  • Speakers with lower averages (Ben, Gina) use more hedged language
  • Box plot width shows tone consistency within each speaker

4 · Topic Modeling

BERTopic with nomic-embed-text and llama3.1

What Is Topic Modeling?

Groups documents by shared patterns — without reading each one individually.

Classical (LDA, NMF)

Groups by shared words

Neural (BERTopic)

Groups by shared meaning, even with different words

Trade-off: neural groupings are based on learned vector representations, so the reasoning behind a cluster is less directly readable from the output.

The Five-Step Pipeline

  1. Embed: nomic-embed-text converts each paragraph to 768 numbers
  2. Reduce: UMAP compresses 768 dimensions to 5
  3. Cluster: HDBSCAN finds dense neighborhoods — no forced equal-sized groups
  4. Extract words: c-TF-IDF finds distinctive words per cluster
  5. Label: llama3.1 writes a human-readable topic name

Walk through each step slowly. The key conceptual jump is from text to numbers (embedding) — everything else flows from that.

The Results

7 topics, 2 outlier paragraphs

Topic Docs Speakers
0 · Transparency and Honesty 15 Alex, Ben, Gina, Linda, Otto
1 · Transparency in Content 8 Ben, Linda, Margot, Otto
2 · Authenticity in Promotion 7 Alex, Ben, Gina, Margot
3 · Responsible Promotion 7 Alex, Margot
4 · Inclusive Representation 4 Linda only
5 · Responsible Data Management 4 Gina only
6 · Honesty in Reviews 3 Alex, Gina, Linda

Interpretive Questions

Topics 0, 1, and 2 are all transparency-adjacent.

Are they meaningfully distinct, or does the model separate them on surface differences?

Topics 4 and 5 each come from only one speaker.

Is this a genuine thematic difference — or individual vocabulary?

These questions require close reading. The pipeline surfaces patterns; it does not resolve them.
