Data Cleaning and Pre-Processing

Before any counting happens, raw text needs to be cleaned and standardized. The choices made at this stage directly shape what the analysis surfaces.

Typical pre-processing steps:

  1. Tokenizing: splitting the text into individual words; each word is called a token
  2. Normalizing: converting to lowercase, removing punctuation and numbers
  3. Filtering stop words: removing high-frequency function words that carry little meaning on their own, such as “the”, “and”, “is”
  4. Lemmatizing: reducing words to their base form so variants count together
TipKey terms

Stop words: function words (articles, prepositions, conjunctions) that appear in nearly every document regardless of topic. Removing them lets more content-bearing words surface. The choice of stop word list affects results: “like” and “just” may need to be added manually for conversational text.

Lemmatization: grouping word variants under a shared base form. “promoting”, “promotes”, and “promoted” all become “promote”. Without lemmatization, these count separately and each appears less frequent than it actually is.

What Frequency Reveals and Conceals

What frequency can show What it misses
Which concepts recur most Tone and evaluation (good vs. bad product)
How vocabulary differs across speakers Sarcasm, hedging, negation
Whether certain topics cluster together Context and sequence
Words used by only one speaker What speakers imply but don’t say

A word that appears 15 times in one interview and once in another is worth a closer look. But frequency alone cannot tell you why it appears or what the speaker thinks about it.

Voyant Tools

Voyant Tools is a browser-based text analysis environment. It requires no installation; you upload text and get immediate visualizations.

Open it at: voyant-tools.org

What Voyant provides:

Tool What it shows
Cirrus (word cloud) Most frequent words at a glance
Trends How word frequency changes across documents
Summary Top terms, document lengths, vocabulary density
Contexts Keywords in context (KWIC): words shown in their surrounding sentences
Collocates Words that consistently appear in the same context as a target word

What Word Counting Adds

Frequency analysis works best as a starting point. It helps you:

  • Identify vocabulary worth tracing across speakers
  • Notice imbalances in who uses what language
  • Generate hypotheses before running more complex analyses

When you move to sentiment analysis and topic modeling, keep the frequency results in mind. Do the dominant topics match the most frequent words? Do speakers with distinctive vocabulary also cluster into distinct topics?
